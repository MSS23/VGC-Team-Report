## 2. How Top Creators Currently Share Teams

### 2a. Wolfey (Wolfe Glick) — The Patreon Funnel Model
Wolfey is the clearest example of a deliberate, multi-channel team-sharing strategy:

- **After every major tournament win**, he posts to Twitter: "I've posted the rental team and EV spreads on Patreon!" — gating full details behind a subscription paywall.
- **Public Twitter:** Posts the win announcement, tournament highlights, and a rental code (for casual players), with minimal EV/IV detail.
- **Patreon (paid):** Full EVs, IVs, items, moves, personal post-event reflections, and explanation of spread choices.
- **Example:** Charlotte Regional 2024 win tweet — 334K views, 9.6K likes, 372 replies. The tweet pointed to Patreon for detailed team info.
- **Key behavioral insight:** Wolfey treats the team reveal as a **content asset** to drive Patreon subscriptions, not as a free community contribution. The "rental code" is the free hook; the real knowledge is gated.

### 2b. Cybertron (Aaron Zheng) — Educational Content + Collaboration
- Writes comprehensive team-building **guides** (e.g., VGC 2020 Team Building Guide) for public consumption, shared freely on Twitter.
- Co-founded **VGCguide.com** with Wolfey and Aaron Traylor — a 100,000-word "timeless" educational resource covering fundamentals.
- Shares content via YouTube/Twitter as educational outreach rather than monetized team reveals.
- Focus is on **growing the audience** via accessible guides, not gating tournament-specific builds.

### 2c. Aaron Traylor — The Medium Long-Form Report Model
- Publishes detailed **tournament reports on Medium** (free, public).
- Report structure: team origin story → individual Pokémon breakdowns → round-by-round game summaries → takeaways.
- Examples: Dallas Regional Champion report, Indianapolis Top 8 report, VGC Worlds Top 16 "warstory."
- Treats team reports as **creative writing** — "warstories" that combine competitive analysis with personal narrative.
- **Pain point implied:** Medium is not purpose-built for VGC content. No damage calc embeds, no inline Pokepaste, no sprite rendering. Readers must copy-paste the paste link separately.

### 2d. James Baek — Streaming + YouTube
- Active Twitch Partner who streams VGC frequently, builds teams live on stream.
- YouTube channel for post-tournament content.
- No evidence of a distinctive Patreon-gated model; appears to share more openly via streaming.

### 2e. Paul Chua — Tournament Result Sharing
- Posts results to Twitter; community sites (Victory Road, VGCpedia, PokéHexDex) host team details after wins.
- 2026 EUIC win covered by Victory Road (@VGCVictoryRoad) with team compilation on community sites.
- Not a high-volume content creator compared to Wolfey/Cybertron; relies on **community infrastructure** to distribute his team info.

### 2f. Phillip Wingett (THATSAplusONE) — Patreon Team Library
- Uses Patreon to share an entire library of VGC teams; recent post: "EVERY VGC Team coming to YouTube for the next couple weeks!"
- 10-step team-building guides are Patreon-exclusive content.
- Treats team builds as the primary **Patreon value proposition**.

## 3. Community Infrastructure for Team Sharing

### VGCPastes (@VGCPastes)
- Volunteer-run Twitter/X account that aggregates Pokepastes from the community.
- Regulation I repository: 63 teams; Regulation H: 1,150+ teams.
- Works with **Falinks Teambuilder** website for browsable web repository.
- Community submits pastes via Twitter DM or Discord.
- **Limitation:** Pastes are raw text — no visual team sheet, no embedded damage calcs, no narrative context.

### Victory Road (victoryroad.pro)
- Primary destination for polished **team reports** — written by players, hosted editorially.
- Also hosts rental team libraries with rental codes.
- Twitter: @VGCVictoryRoad — posts tournament results and team compilations.
- Reports include importable paste + qualitative analysis.

### Nimbasa City Post (nimbasacitypost.com)
- Team reports, news, analysis; includes an updated VGC damage calculator.
- More editorial/blog format.

### MetaGame (metagamevgc.com)
- Dedicated team reports section with importable team versions.

### Nugget Bridge (nuggetbridge.com)
- Legacy community site; early home of team report culture.

### Limitless VGC (limitlessvgc.com)
- Tournament database with team lists from official events.

### VGC.tools (vgc.tools)
- Community-driven team builder and public team library. 
- Build, share, browse, and learn from teams.

### VGC Helper (vgchelper.com)
- Mobile-friendly team builder.
- Added **team preview image sharing** in v1.2.0 — teams can be exported as a shareable image, a significant UX step forward.

### Pikalytics (pikalytics.com)
- Usage stat aggregator + team builder.
- Allows copying, sharing, and image-sharing teams.

## 4. What Makes a Team-Sharing Moment Go Viral

Based on research patterns across the VGC community:

### The Tournament Win Announcement Loop
1. Player wins a major event (Regional, International, Worlds).
2. Player posts a **win tweet** with emotional context ("incredibly proud," "largest official tournament ever").
3. Community infrastructure (Victory Road, VGCPastes) aggregates and disseminates the team data.
4. Content creators post YouTube/Twitch content breaking down the team.
5. The **rental code** drives casual engagement (anyone can use the team immediately).

**Wolfey's Charlotte 2024 win tweet** is the clearest viral example: 334K views, 9.6K likes — driven by milestone framing (largest tournament ever, most sets ever won), not just the team itself.

### What Amplifies Engagement
- **Milestone framing:** Record-breaking achievement creates news hooks.
- **Accessible rental code:** Lowers barrier — fans can literally play the team.
- **Mystery/exclusivity:** Full EVs behind Patreon paywall creates aspiration and FOMO.
- **Community controversy:** The "team stealing" incident (Wolfey's team "leaked" before NAIC) generated enormous engagement (community debate, multiple threads, Dot Esports coverage). Team ownership drama drives clicks.
- **Collaboration reveals:** VGCguide launch (Wolfey + Cybertron + Traylor) — collaborative announcements get amplified by multiple audiences simultaneously.

### The "Team Stealing" Discourse
- Wolfey publicly called out another player (Brady Smith) for bringing a similar team, calling it "stolen/leaked."
- Community pushed back: "everyone steals teams / nobody's hiding anything."
- This generated **hate campaigns, community drama, and mainstream esports coverage** (Dot Esports).
- **Insight for VGC Team Report app:** Team attribution and original-creator credit is a hot-button issue in the community. Any sharing tool must handle attribution carefully.

## 5. Creator Pain Points with Existing Tools

### Pain Point 1: No Purpose-Built Team Report Canvas
- Long-form reports are written on **Medium** (Aaron Traylor) or hosted on **Victory Road** (editorially gated). Neither is self-serve.
- There is no tool where a player can write a structured report *alongside* the team data in one integrated experience.

### Pain Point 2: Pokepaste is Utilitarian, Not Shareable
- Pokepaste (pokepast.es) is pure text — no visuals, no Pokémon sprites, no damage calcs, no matchup context.
- VGCHelper added image export as a notable feature (v1.2.0), signaling demand for visual team sharing.
- For social media (Twitter's image-first feed), a text paste is a dead end — it doesn't render engagingly.

### Pain Point 3: Patreon Friction for Knowledge Gating
- Top creators (Wolfey, Wingett) gate EV/IV/spread data behind Patreon. This is a workaround, not a purpose-built solution.
- No tool lets creators publish a **tiered team report** — public team shell + paywalled spread detail.

### Pain Point 4: Team Attribution / Credit Infrastructure
- The community credits team origins informally and imprecisely.
- There is no canonical "this is my original team" record-keeping — which fuels the team stealing discourse.
- VGCPastes credits creators manually; there's no systemic attribution at the paste layer.

### Pain Point 5: Fragmented Discovery
- Teams live across: VGCPastes (Twitter), Victory Road (website), Limitless (tournament DB), VGC.tools (team library), Patreon (paywalled), Medium (long-form), YouTube (video), Discord (chat).
- No single discovery surface. Fans must follow many accounts and sites to find the best builds.

### Pain Point 6: No Battle-to-Report Pipeline
- Players must manually reconstruct their tournament run in a report; there's no integration between replay/tournament tools and team report writing.

## 7. Emerging Tools (2025–2026)

- **VGC.tools:** Community team library — nearest thing to an integrated build+share platform.
- **VGCHelper v1.2.0:** Added image export for team sheets — addresses Twitter/social sharing need.
- **Pikalytics team builder:** Data-driven builder with usage context baked in.
- **NuzTools VGC Team Builder (Beta):** New entrant, tournament-sheet focused.
- **Pokemon Champions team sharing board (Game8):** In-game replica ID (rental code) as the share unit.
- **pokemonvgcteamreport.com:** Dedicated team-report site — appears to be a direct competitor/precursor to the VGC Team Report app being built.

---

## 8. Strategic Implications for VGC Team Report App

1. **The viral moment is the win announcement** — the app needs to make that post *effortless and beautiful* immediately after a tournament result.
2. **Rental codes are the free viral hook** — embedding rental codes prominently will drive casual shares.
3. **Attribution infrastructure is a community need** — solving the "who made this team" problem would be welcomed.
4. **Creator monetization integration** — a Patreon-style tiered report (public shell + private spreads) would attract top creators.
5. **Image-first sharing** — Twitter/X is image-first; the app's team visualization needs to be screenshot-worthy out of the box.
6. **Long-form report canvas** — Medium + Victory Road combined: a structured, purpose-built report editor where paste, spreads, matchup notes, and narrative all live together.
7. **Discovery layer** — aggregating pastes from VGCPastes + Victory Road + tournament results in one browsable library would fill a clear gap.
