## 1. Regulation F

### Format Overview
Regulation F covered the 2023 competitive season through much of 2024. It was a Restricted format allowing one Legendary or Mythical Pokemon from a defined restricted list per team, paired with five Pokemon from the full Paldea regional Pokédex plus Home transfers.

### Key Pokemon Allowed / Banned
**Restricted picks (one per team):** Koraidon, Miraidon, Zacian (Hero), Zamazenta (Hero), Eternatus, Calyrex-Ice Rider, Calyrex-Shadow Rider, Kyogre, Groudon, Yveltal, Xerneas, Lunala, Solgaleo, Necrozma forms, Zekrom, Reshiram, Kyurem forms, Palkia-Origin, Dialga-Origin, Giratina-Origin, Rayquaza, Ho-Oh, Lugia, Mewtwo, Raikou, Entei, Suicune

**Notable support Pokemon:** Tornadus, Incineroar, Amoonguss, Rillaboom, Flutter Mane, Iron Hands, Urshifu-Rapid Strike, Farigiraf, Indeedee-F, Chi-Yu

**Banned/Unavailable:** Any Pokemon not on the specific Series list; forms not specifically permitted

### Meta Archetypes That Dominated
- **Miraidon Offense** — Miraidon + Electric Terrain, often paired with Flutter Mane and Iron Hands for strong Electric-boosted offense
- **Koraidon Sun** — Koraidon's Sun-setting ability paired with Flutter Mane, Chi-Yu, or Roaring Moon for powerful Fire and Fairy offense
- **Calyrex-Shadow Rider** — Ghostly fast restricted paired with Tornadus Tailwind support and Grimmsnarl screens
- **Zacian Fairy Offense** — Classic wide guard + Behemoth Blade pressure paired with Amoonguss spore denial

## 2. Regulation G

### Format Overview
Regulation G introduced significant changes to the restricted Pokemon pool, expanding options and rotating out some previously dominant choices. It ran alongside the 2024 season and into major Championship events, including Worlds 2024 preparation.

### Key Pokemon Allowed / Banned
**Restricted shifts:** Certain Legendary Pokemon were added or removed relative to Reg F. The full Home-transferred pool remained available. Key additions included a broader set of Generation 8 and 9 Legendary Pokemon.

**Notable support Pokemon:** Unchanged core from Reg F — Incineroar, Amoonguss, Flutter Mane, and Urshifu-R remained staples. Comfey, Tatsugiri + Dondozo, and Pelipper Rain builds became more prominent.

**Notable changes from Reg F:** Iron Bundle and Flutter Mane usage patterns shifted as specific restricted pairings changed optimal support choices.

### Meta Archetypes That Dominated
- **Tatsugiri + Dondozo** — The "sushi combo" dominated early Reg G with Dondozo at +2/+2/+2/+2 after one Order Up, near-impossible to OHKO with standard damage calcs
- **Rain offense** — Pelipper-Palafin-H rain teams became consistent top performers
- **Incineroar pivoting** — Even more central to Reg G meta as restricted choices diversified
- **Screens HO** — Grimmsnarl screens + Miraidon or Flutter Mane sweeper cores

## 3. Regulation H

### Format Overview
Regulation H marked the shift to Generation 9 (Scarlet/Violet) formats only, removing the ability to use transferred Pokemon from previous generations. This fundamentally reset the metagame: no Incineroar, no Rillaboom, no Urshifu forms, no Flutter Mane from Home. The format used the full Paldea Pokédex plus Kitakami and Blueberry DLC Pokemon, without any restricted Legendaries.

### Key Pokemon Allowed / Banned
**Available (selected highlights):** Arcanine, Torkoal, Amoonguss, Murkrow, Whimsicott, Ninetales-Alola (via DLC), Dondozo, Tatsugiri, Iron Hands, Gholdengo, Palafin-Hero, Roaring Moon, Iron Valiant, Chi-Yu, Chien-Pao, Walking Wake, Iron Leaves, Ogerpon forms, Terapagos

**Not available:** Any Home-transferred Pokemon (Incineroar, Landorus, Rillaboom, Urshifu, Flutter Mane when transferred, etc.)

### Meta Archetypes That Dominated
- **Torkoal + Ninetales-A Sun** — With Incineroar gone, dedicated Sun became viable; Torkoal Drought + Ogerpon-Wellspring or Arcanine offense
- **Ogerpon-Wellspring** — Water-type Ogerpon with Ivy Cudgel became an S-tier threat in multiple archetypes
- **Terapagos + Stellar Tera** — Terapagos Terastalizing into Stellar type with Tera Starstorm was a central restricted-level threat
- **Chien-Pao Offense** — Sword of Ruin + Ruin passive abilities coordinating with hard hitters like Gholdengo

## 4. Regulation I

### Format Overview
Regulation I is the current (as of 2025–2026) standard format. It builds on Reg H's Paldea-only base while reintroducing select Home Pokemon on an updated approved list. The format has been the primary format for the 2025 World Championships cycle. One restricted Legendary Pokemon is permitted per team.

### Key Pokemon Allowed / Banned
**Restricted (current list — verify against official Pokemon ruleset before publishing):** Selected from the Scarlet/Violet Legendary pool including Koraidon, Miraidon, Terapagos, Ogerpon forms (Wellspring, Hearthflame, Cornerstone), and others per the current Series rules.

**Home returns:** Selected Pokemon returned from previous generations on the approved list — verify against official list at time of writing.

**Key support options:** Amoonguss, Murkrow, Whimsicott, Arcanine, Torkoal, Ninetales-A, Dondozo+Tatsugiri remain strong. Farigiraf, Indeedee-F, and Comfey provide redirection and Trick Room support.

### Meta Archetypes That Dominated
- **Miraidon + Pelipper Weather Wars** — Electric Terrain vs Rain matchups became a central skill-test

- **Balance Gholdengo** — Gholdengo as an uncounterable Trick Room check and spreader
- **Restricted Stall** — Calyrex-Ice in Trick Room with Murkrow Tailwind denial

## 5. Champions Series

### Format Overview
The VGC Champions Series refers to the post-Worlds, high-level competitive series events including Regional Championships, International Championships, and the World Championships themselves. The Champions Series uses whatever the current active Regulation is, but at these events the field is the highest level of global competition — the strategies and team choices here define the true meta ceiling.

This guide covers Champions Series-specific strategy context rather than format rules (which match the active Regulation guide).

### Key Considerations for Champions Series Play
- **Limited rounds, higher stakes:** Tournament structure rewards consistent, safe play over high-variance strategies
- **Scouting is real:** Top players research opponents' known teams; tech choices and concealed movesets carry extra value
- **Best-of-3 matters:** Teams need depth — strategies that are strong in Game 1 but transparent in Game 2 are punished heavily
- **Speed ties and coin flips are less acceptable:** At this level, spreads are tuned to avoid speed ties and reduce reliance on low-accuracy moves

### Meta Archetypes Common at Major Events
- **Defensive balance cores** — Top players often run teams that can answer multiple threats rather than pure offensive builds
- **Anti-meta tech** — Niche items (Lum Berry, Safety Goggles), unusual spreads, or coverage moves specifically targeting expected field
- **Lead flexibility** — Teams with multiple viable lead combinations are preferred at Champions Series level
