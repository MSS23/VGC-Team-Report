## 1. Rain

### How the Archetype Works
Rain teams set permanent or turn-limited Rain weather (via Drizzle ability or Rain Dance) to power up Water-type moves by 50% and give Swift Swim users doubled Speed. The goal is to overwhelm opponents with fast, powerful Water attackers before they can establish their own win condition. Pelipper is the universal Drizzle setter in modern VGC; Palafin-Hero and Barraskewda are the primary Swift Swim abusers.

A typical Rain game plan: lead Pelipper + a Rain abuser. Pelipper sets weather, provides Hurricane as a spread move, and uses Wide Guard or Tailwind as needed. The abuser threatens to OHKO or 2HKO most of the field under Rain at doubled Speed.

### Key Pokemon
- **Pelipper** — Drizzle setter, Hurricane spread move, Tailwind support, Wide Guard
- **Palafin-Hero** — Supreme Overlord + Wave Crash off massive Attack stat; the Rain win condition
- **Barraskewda** — Choice Band Swift Swim physical sweeper; faster than Palafin but less powerful
- **Ludicolo** — Special Rain abuser with Giga Drain and Thunder coverage; less common but adds type diversity
- **Tornadus** — Tailwind support, Prankster Taunt, Bleakwind Storm off-weather damage
- **Amoonguss** — Rage Powder redirection to protect Pelipper; Spore for speed control

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- **Pelipper:** Bulky enough to survive key hits from non-super-effective attacks; Speed investment not needed (sets weather turn 1, doesn't need to outspeed). Common EV benchmark: survive a non-boosted Iron Hands Fake Out.
- **Palafin-Hero:** Maximum Attack and Speed. Sometimes 252/252 Jolly or Adamant depending on what threats need to be outsped.
- **Support Pokemon (Tornadus, Amoonguss):** Split between bulk and speed depending on role — Tornadus often Choice Scarf or max Speed; Amoonguss usually max HP with spDef investment.

### Strengths
- Very high damage ceiling under Rain — even defensive teams can be overwhelmed in a single turn
- Rain is self-sustaining (Pelipper just needs to stay alive)
- Wide Guard covers Earthquake and Surf chip damage from opponents
- Flexible leads — Pelipper pairs well with almost any Rain abuser

### Weaknesses
- Hard countered by strong weather war teams (Torkoal Sun can Weather-override; Sandstorm reduces special damage)
- Vulnerable to Trick Room — Swift Swim users become the slowest Pokemon in TR
- Heavily reliant on Pelipper staying alive; Pelipper is frail
- Predictable lead pattern gives opponents information and allows prepared counterplay

## 2. Sun

### How the Archetype Works
Sun teams use Drought (Torkoal in current Reg H/I formats) to boost Fire-type moves by 50%, power up Solar Blade and SolarBeam to one-turn charging, and disable Thunder, Blizzard accuracy. In Reg H/I specifically, Ninetales-Alolan provides Aurora Veil + Snow rather than competing Sun, so the Sun setter is almost always Torkoal. The core offensive synergy is Torkoal + a Fire/Grass attacker — often Ogerpon-Hearthflame or Arcanine.

The secondary Sun benefit is neutralizing opposing Rain (weather wars) and enabling Chlorophyll users like Venusaur to double their Speed.

### Key Pokemon
- **Torkoal** — Drought setter, Shell Smash on some sets, Eruption as a powerful spread move under Sun
- **Ogerpon-Hearthflame** — Hearthflame Mask boosts Fire Ivy Cudgel; extremely difficult to check under Sun
- **Arcanine** — Intimidate + Will-O-Wisp spread support; Extreme Speed priority; offensive Sun abuser
- **Venusaur** — Chlorophyll doubles Speed under Sun; Sleep Powder + Earth Power coverage
- **Flutter Mane (if available via Home)** — Moonblast + Shadow Ball spread; fast and hits hard even without Sun boost
- **Chi-Yu** — Beads of Ruin + Overheat under Sun; devastating special Fire damage

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- **Torkoal:** Usually max HP with defensive investment — it doesn't attack often but needs to survive to keep Sun up. Speed EVs rarely invested beyond specific benchmarks.
- **Ogerpon-Hearthflame:** Max Attack, max Speed Jolly or Adamant depending on the speed tier targeted. Often needs to outspeed specific threats.
- **Arcanine:** Bulkier spreads to abuse Intimidate cycling. Sometimes Assault Vest for special bulk.

### Strengths
- Weather control gives the team a passive advantage throughout the game
- Ogerpon-Hearthflame is extremely difficult to OHKO with most typical coverage
- Sun counters Rain, which is one of the most popular archetypes
- Multiple viable offensive Pokemon that all benefit from the same weather

### Weaknesses
- Torkoal is slow and frail — KO it and the team loses its engine
- Rock-type moves are super effective on both Torkoal and Arcanine
- Water-type moves threaten the core Fire attackers even under Sun
- Rain teams can override Sun with Drizzle; weather wars are often decided by who can KO the setter first

## 3. Trick Room

### How the Archetype Works
Trick Room reverses the turn order for 5 turns — the slowest Pokemon moves first. Trick Room teams invest in minimum Speed (0 IVs, -Speed nature) on their attackers and set Trick Room via a fast support Pokemon or a dedicated Trick Room setter with redirection support. The key challenge is getting Trick Room up safely against opponents who will always try to prevent or KO the setter.

Standard game plan: Lead setter + Rage Powder/Follow Me redirector. Set Trick Room on Turn 1. Bring in the attacker. Win by moving first while being extremely slow.

### Key Pokemon
- **Farigiraf** — Cud Chew + Trick Room; can set TR multiple times; Psychic noise denies Heal Bell
- **Indeedee-F** — Psychic Surge sets Psychic Terrain (blocks priority); Follow Me redirection; sets Trick Room
- **Dondozo** — Slow physical attacker with enormous HP; pairs with Tatsugiri for Order Up boosts
- **Amoonguss** — Rage Powder + Spore combination; minimum Speed Amoonguss "outspeeds" everything in TR
- **Iron Hands** — Not traditionally a TR team member, but bulky enough to function; Drain Punch + Fake Out
- **Gholdengo** — Good as Gold blocks status moves targeting it; works in or out of TR

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- **Setters (Farigiraf, Indeedee-F):** Max HP bulk. Minimum Speed investment — they need to be slow to function inside their own TR, or fast enough to set it before the opponent acts.
- **Attackers (Dondozo, slow Tera Pokemon):** 0 Speed IVs, -Speed nature (Brave, Quiet, Relaxed, Sassy). Maximum offensive stat. HP investment for bulk.
- **Redirectors:** Max HP, redirection ability — that's the spread. Speed doesn't matter.

### Strengths
- Demolishes fast offense archetypes that have no way to outspeed Trick Room attackers
- Multiple setters allow redundancy — KO one and another takes over
- High floor — if TR lands, the attacker often gets multiple turns of free damage
- Psychic Surge from Indeedee-F removes Fake Out as an option for opponents

### Weaknesses
- Vulnerable to Taunt (blocks Trick Room entirely if the setter is Taunted first)
- Tailwind from opponent extends their own speed control and wins speed wars
- Trick Room has a 5-turn timer — one turn of stalling (switching, Protect) eats into it
- Weak to opposing Trick Room — mirror matches require knowing when not to reset TR

## 4. Hyper Offense

### How the Archetype Works
Hyper Offense (HO) sacrifices defensive depth entirely for maximum immediate damage output. Every team member hits as hard as possible, as fast as possible. The goal is to KO two opposing Pokemon before the opponent can establish any game plan. HO teams often use Tailwind to extend their speed advantage, Screens (Light Screen + Reflect via Grimmsnarl or Arcanine) to survive one extra hit, or Choice items to maximize damage.

HO does not play defensively — if an attack doesn't KO, it accepts that trade and hits harder next turn.

### Key Pokemon
- **Grimmsnarl** — Prankster Reflect + Light Screen; buys HO attackers one extra hit of survival
- **Flutter Mane (when available)** — Fastest and strongest Fairy/Ghost attacker in the format
- **Chi-Yu** — Beads of Ruin drops Special Defense of all non-Chi-Yu Pokemon; devastating spread damage
- **Chien-Pao** — Sword of Ruin drops Defense; fast physical STAB with Ice Fang and Sacred Sword coverage
- **Iron Valiant** — Moonblast + Close Combat coverage; Booster Energy for +1 Speed in Electric Terrain
- **Roaring Moon** — Dragon/Dark Dragon Dance sweeper; hits hard after a single boost

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- Full offence investment: 252 in primary attacking stat + 252 Speed in most cases
- Nature should be maximising the primary stat (Timid for special attackers, Jolly for physical, or Modest/Adamant if running specific speed benchmarks)
- Minimal bulk — if a Pokemon is here it's because it hits hard, not because it survives
- Items: Life Orb, Choice Specs, Choice Band, or Booster Energy depending on the Pokemon

### Strengths
- Can end games in 1–2 turns before defensive teams can set up
- Very high damage ceiling — nothing is safe to leave in
- Each Pokemon is a threat individually, requiring the opponent to answer every single one

### Weaknesses
- Falls apart against Trick Room — all HO attackers become last to move
- Weak to Intimidate cycling; physical HO especially suffers
- No safety net — if an OHKO fails, HO teams have no recovery plan
- Becomes predictable over a Best-of-3 series; opponents adjust

## 5. Balance

### How the Archetype Works
Balance teams combine offensive threat with defensive utility — usually 2–3 hard hitters supported by 2–3 utility Pokemon that handle speed control, redirection, and chip denial. Balance teams aim to answer most common threats without hard countering any one thing. They win by playing flexibly and making better decisions than the opponent over 5–10 turns, rather than winning in the first 2.

Balance is the most common archetype at top-level events because it has no catastrophic hard counters and rewards high-skill play.

### Key Pokemon
- **Incineroar (when available)** — Intimidate, Fake Out, Parting Shot: the definition of defensive utility
- **Amoonguss** — Rage Powder + Spore; slows the opponent without Trick Room
- **Arcanine** — Intimidate cycling + Will-O-Wisp; reduces physical damage of multiple attackers
- **Tornadus** — Tailwind setter + Prankster Taunt; flexible speed control
- **Gholdengo** — Status immunity from Good as Gold; offensive threat that doesn't require setup
- **Farigiraf / Indeedee-F** — Terrain / Trick Room option that gives the team a surprise second gear

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- Balance teams tune spreads to specific damage benchmarks rather than maxing stats
- Example: An Arcanine spread might be "survives a Choice Specs Flutter Mane Moonblast while retaining enough offensive output to threaten OHKO with Extreme Speed on 75% HP targets"
- Speed control Pokemon often run creep spreads — a few extra Speed EVs to beat other common creep benchmarks
- HP investment over raw defensive stats is common to maximise overall bulk

### Strengths
- Flexible game plans — can play through Trick Room, Rain, or Sun matchups without a single dedicated answer
- Best-of-3 adaptability: pivot strategies mid-series based on what the opponent shows
- Less reliant on any one Pokemon — KO Incineroar and Arcanine or Amoonguss continues the defensive core

### Weaknesses
- Lower damage ceiling than HO — can struggle to KO bulky restricted Legendaries in one hit
- Slow to establish win conditions; HO teams can outpace before Balance stabilises
- Requires high decision-making quality — a Balance team in the hands of an inexperienced player underperforms relative to a simpler HO team

## 6. Stall

### How the Archetype Works
Stall teams win by never losing — they aim to outlast the opponent using recovery moves, Protect stalling, passive damage (poison, burn, sandstorm chip), and extremely high defensive investment. Victory comes from the opponent eventually running out of PP or making an error under pressure. In VGC doubles format, true Stall is rare and generally considered unviable at top levels — the 2-Pokemon-per-side format makes it far harder to wall threats than in singles.

"Stall" in VGC more accurately describes defensive anchor strategies: Pokemon that are very hard to KO and provide utility turns by tanking attacks rather than attacking. Full Stall in the Singles sense is not a top-tier VGC archetype.

### Key Pokemon
- **Dondozo** — Without an Order Up boost, Dondozo is extremely bulky; Encore + Protect stalling is a legitimate stall loop
- **Calyrex-Ice Rider** — Defensive restricted attacker; not fast but nearly impossible to OHKO
- **Blissey / Chansey (if available)** — Pure special sponge; Soft-Boiled + toxic stall
- **Murkrow** — Prankster Tailwind, Haze to reset boosts; not a stall Pokemon but enables stall by removing opponent setup
- **Umbreon** — Wish + Protect recovery cycling with Foul Play
- **Amoonguss** — Regenerator + Rage Powder passive recovery; not stall-specific but fulfills a defensive anchor role

### Typical EV Spread Philosophy
- Maximum HP investment in almost all cases
- Defensive stats split depending on the threat being walled (physical or special)
- No Speed investment — stall Pokemon want to move last to Protect after observing attacks
- Leftovers or Black Sludge for passive recovery are essential items

### Strengths
- Hard to KO in any single turn — creates tempo advantages by surviving attacks others would not
- Passive damage + recovery wins long games that offensive teams can't close
- Demoralizing to play against — opponent must commit to specific KO sequences

### Weaknesses
- Very weak to the 8-turn timer in VGC series — matches time out, stall can lose by timer in close situations
- Taunt completely disables recovery moves
- Trick Room stall specifically loses to fast Taunt users
- The aggressive pace of VGC doubles generally punishes stall — two opponents attacking simultaneously makes passive recovery much less reliable than in singles
