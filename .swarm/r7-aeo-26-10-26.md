## 1. Headline findings (read these five, skip the rest if short on time)

1. **Our homepage serves 91 characters of body text.** `CODE` The prerendered `/` is 38,959 bytes of HTML containing **zero `<h1>`, zero `<h2>`**, and the only visible text is `"VGC Team Report - Build and Share Pokemon VGC Teams | Skip to content | Loading team builder..."`. The entire page is behind a client `Suspense`. This is the page that would answer "best VGC team builder" and "VGC team report tool". To a non-JS crawler it is blank. **This single fact explains most of the non-citation.**
2. **robots.txt is correct and permissive** `CODE` — all five AI crawlers are allowed. This is *not* the problem, and the v5.27 fix held. Details in §3.
3. **`llms.txt` exists and is good. `llms-full.txt` exists and is actively harmful** `CODE` — it teaches AI crawlers the exact "1 SP = 1 EV / Standard Points" myth that VGC-266 was filed to kill. The regression test guarding that myth only reads `llms.txt`; `llms-full.txt` slipped through the gate. Details in §4.

An engine that retrieves two of our pages gets a contradiction and will prefer a source that doesn't contradict itself.
5. **We advertise an embed feature that cannot work.** `CODE` Sitewide CSP sets `frame-ancestors 'none'` plus `X-Frame-Options: DENY` on `source: "/(.*)"`, with no override for `/embed/[id]` and no middleware. `llms-full.txt` and the `/faq` HowTo both tell users (and AI assistants) to copy an iframe snippet. ### 2.3 The structural reason we lose

`INFERENCE`, grounded in the CODE findings and corroborated by `MEDIUM` sources:

Multiple practitioner sources agree that **GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot have limited or no JavaScript execution**, while Google AI Overviews and Bing/Copilot render well. A crawler that doesn't run JS sees an empty container where content should be. Our homepage *is* that empty container (§1.1). So:

- For **brand/entity queries** ("VGC team report tool") the engine fetches `/` as the canonical entity page, gets 91 characters, and has nothing to extract — no description, no feature list in prose, no h1 to match the query. It falls back to whatever third-party page mentions us, and §2.1 shows there effectively isn't one.
- For **category queries** ("best VGC team builder") we never enter the candidate set, because entering it requires either third-party mentions (we have ~none) or a page whose server-rendered prose states the category answer (we have none on `/`).

The two things that *are* working: `/faq` (7,758 visible chars, 1 h1 + 13 h2) and `/tools/ev-to-sp` (7,135 visible chars, h1 + 6 h2 + 7 h3) are genuinely well-built AEO pages. They are just not the pages an engine lands on first, and nothing links to them from outside.

### 2.4 Two corrections to the prior R7 strategy

The previous R7 reports in `.swarm/` (notably `r7-aeo-audit.md`, 2026-05-28) leaned heavily on `llms.txt` as the lever. Two things have changed:

- **`llms.txt` has no measured effect on AI citations.** `MEDIUM` A practitioner guide citing four independent studies (Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Trakkr, and Google's own June 2026 Search Central guidance) reports no measurable lift, and states Google Search ignores the file outright. I could not verify the primary sources from this container (developers.google.com unreachable), so treat the specific attributions as **LOW CONFIDENCE** — but the direction is consistent across sources. **Keep `llms.txt` as cheap insurance; stop treating it as the strategy.**
- **FAQPage rich results are gone.** `HIGH` Google's 2023-08-08 Search Central post limited FAQ rich results to government and health sites, and its FAQPage documentation now says the search appearance, rich-result report and Rich Results Test support were dropped in **June 2026** (sources disagree between May and August 2026 for the exact milestones — `MEDIUM`). FAQPage markup is now worth having *only* as an extraction aid for AI engines. Which means: **schema emitted with no matching visible prose is now worth approximately zero.** That reframes items 6 and 7 below from "nice to have" to "we are shipping dead weight".

## 3. robots.txt audit — PASS

`public/robots.txt`, verbatim `CODE`:

```
# A named User-agent group REPLACES the * group entirely (no inheritance),
# so per-bot "Allow: /" groups silently un-blocked /api/ for those bots.
# Every crawler already gets Allow: / from the wildcard — one group is both
# sufficient and the only way the Disallow applies to everyone. (VGC-272)
User-agent: *
Allow: /
Disallow: /api/

Sitemap: https://pokemonvgcteamreport.com/sitemap.xml
```

**Stance on the five crawlers named in the brief:**

| Crawler | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| GPTBot (OpenAI training) | **Allowed** | falls under `User-agent: *` |
| ClaudeBot (Anthropic) | **Allowed** | falls under `*` |
| PerplexityBot | **Allowed** | falls under `*` |
| Google-Extended (Gemini / AI Overviews grounding) | **Allowed** | falls under `*` |
| CCBot (Common Crawl) | **Allowed** | falls under `*` |

`/api/` is disallowed for everyone, which is correct and costs nothing (no answer content lives there). **The v5.27 correction is verified and the reasoning in the comment is right** — a named `User-agent` group does replace the `*` group with no inheritance, so the earlier per-bot `Allow: /` blocks really were un-disallowing `/api/`. Do not "improve" this file by re-adding named groups.

Two things are **not** in robots.txt and should be:
- No `llms.txt` pointer. There is no standard directive for it, but a comment line plus a `<link>` in `<head>` is how the file gets discovered at all (§4.3).
- `/compare` is `noindex` at the page level and correctly excluded from the sitemap `CODE`, but is still crawlable. That is fine — leave it.

Also verified: **no `src/app/robots.ts`** exists, so there is no route handler shadowing the static file; and `next.config.ts` sets no `X-Robots-Tag` anywhere `CODE`. Nothing is quietly noindexing the site.

## 4. llms.txt audit — one good file, one harmful file

### 4.1 `public/llms.txt` (3,532 bytes) — GOOD, with fixable staleness

This is a well-formed llmstxt.org-style file: a one-paragraph entity summary that explicitly differentiates us from PokePaste and Pikalytics, a "What makes it different" list, a "Main URLs" list with a one-line purpose per URL, and a "Key concepts" glossary. It answers "what is this and how is it different" cleanly. **The SP section is correct and matches the code:**

> **SP (Stat Points) vs EVs:** SP is the stat-investment system used by Pokémon Champions (Regulation M-A/M-B/M-C) in place of EVs. The conversion is not 1:1 — the first SP in a stat costs 4 EVs and every SP after that costs 8 (so 32 SP = 248+ EVs). The budget is 66 SP total per Pokémon with a maximum of 32 SP per stat…

That matches `CHAMPIONS_TOTAL_SP = 66` and `CHAMPIONS_MAX_SP_PER_STAT = 32` in `src/lib/analysis/stat-calculator.ts`, and it is pinned by `src/lib/analysis/__tests__/sp-docs-drift.test.ts` `CODE`. Good engineering.

Gaps:
- **`Updated: 2026-05-23`** — five months stale. The *content* was clearly edited since (it describes Reg M-C, Sep 9 – Dec 2 2026, which shipped in v5.28 in October) but the date stamp was not bumped. A recency-weighting engine reads a five-month-old stamp on current content and discounts it. `INFERENCE`, but the cost of fixing is one line.
- **`/tools/ev-to-sp` is missing from "Main URLs"** — our single best AEO asset, in the sitemap at priority 0.8, absent from the file we hand to AI assistants. `CODE`
- **Nothing answers "how do I share my VGC team"** as a question. The file says sharing exists; it never gives the steps. The `/faq` HowTo steps exist in the repo and should be inlined here.
- No mention of the free OTS generator, the `/compare` tool, or forking — all real differentiators.

### 4.2 `public/llms-full.txt` (9,302 bytes) — ACTIVELY HARMFUL, fix first

Same header date. Four defects, in severity order:

**(a) It teaches the exact myth VGC-266 killed.** Verbatim `CODE`:

> **SP (Stat Points or Standard Points)** is an alternative notation sometimes used in team reports and competitive coaching content — particularly in some international communities — where 1 SP = 1 EV. The terms are interchangeable. If you see a Pokémon listed with "252 SP Atk" it means the same thing as "252 EVs in Attack" (i.e., 252 Attack EVs).
> VGC Team Report accepts both notations when importing and displays EVs in the standard Showdown format on all report pages.

Every sentence is wrong, and it is wrong in the long-form file an assistant reads when it wants depth. The guard test only reads `llms.txt`:

```ts
const llms = readFileSync(join(process.cwd(), "public", "llms.txt"), "utf8");
…
expect(llms).not.toMatch(/standard points/i);
expect(llms).not.toMatch(/1 SP = 1 EV/i);
```

`llms-full.txt` was never added to that assertion, so the regression survived the fix. This is the highest-severity content bug on the site: we are the self-declared authority on Champions SP, and our deepest AI-facing document contradicts our own calculator, our own `/faq`, our own `/tools/ev-to-sp`, and Bulbapedia.

**(b) Restricted-legendary contradiction across four surfaces.** `CODE`

| Surface | Says |
|---|---|
| `public/llms-full.txt` | "Two restricted Legendary Pokémon are allowed (same as Regulation G/H)" |
| `src/app/faq/page.tsx` (Reg M-C answer) | "restricted legendaries are banned" |
| `src/components/seo/JsonLd.tsx` `DEFAULT_FAQ_ITEMS` — **shipped on the homepage** | "Restricted legendaries are banned" |
| `src/lib/validation/champions-legality.ts` | `Max 2 restricted Pokemon`; errors only at `> 2` |

I am **not** adjudicating the real rule — I can't verify it from this container and it is a format question for a human. The finding is that **we ship three different answers and the code implements a fourth position**, and two of those surfaces are the ones AI engines read. A human who knows the Reg M-C rule needs to pick one and then all four must be reconciled, with a drift test like the SP one.

**(c) The embed feature it documents is blocked by our own CSP.** `llms-full.txt` ends its sharing section with:

> Use **Embed** to get an `<iframe>` snippet… The embed URL follows the pattern `https://pokemonvgcteamreport.com/embed/[id]`.

`next.config.ts` applies to `source: "/(.*)"`, unconditionally `CODE`:

```

…
```

There is no `/embed` header exception, no `src/middleware.ts`, and `src/app/embed/[id]/page.tsx` sets only `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` — nothing that could relax a response header. `/faq`'s HowTo step 5 repeats the same advice. **High confidence this is broken in every browser**; a human should confirm against prod before the copy is changed, because the right fix might be to make the embed work rather than to delete the claim.

**(d) Minor factual drift:** "maximum of 508 total EVs" (the cap is 510, of which 508 are usable, and `llms.txt` itself says 510); the Champions section is written as if M-A were current and only mentions M-B/M-C in an addendum; and it describes `/explore` filtering and `/tournaments` content a crawler cannot verify (see §5.3).
