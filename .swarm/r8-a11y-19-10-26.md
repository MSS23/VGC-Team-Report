### Headings

#### F-1 · Edit-mode slide 0 has no `<h1>`, and its first heading is an `<h3>`
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships` · `2.4.6 Headings and Labels`
`src/components/report/TeamOverview.tsx:418-436` and `:531`; gate at `src/components/home/HomePageClient.tsx:1150`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: XS · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`TeamOverview.tsx`)

`isReadOnly = isSharedReadOnly || presentationMode || !creatorMode` (`src/hooks/useHomePage.ts:529`), so
`isReadOnly === false` is exactly edit mode. In that state `TeamOverview` takes the `else` branch and both
of its `<h1>`s are skipped — the `sr-only` one at `:419` is gated on `isReadOnly && !teamName`, the visible
one at `:432` sits inside the `isReadOnly ?` arm. `HomePageClient`'s compensating `sr-only` `<h1>` is gated on
`physicalSlide !== 0`, so it does not fire either. The first heading a creator's screen reader meets is the
`<h3>` "Team Details" at `:531`.

*A creator editing their own report hears no page title at all and lands two levels down the heading outline.*

**Fix:** move the `sr-only` `<h1>` at `:419` out of the `isReadOnly` branch so it renders whenever no visible
`<h1>` does — i.e. gate it on `!(isReadOnly && teamName)`. One-line change; no new markup.

#### F-2 · Heading levels skip h2 on the three analysis slides
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships`
`OffensiveCoverageChart.tsx:168`, `DefensiveCoverageChart.tsx:72`, `ThreatMatrix.tsx:105`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: XS · conflict risk: low

`HomePageClient` supplies the `sr-only` `<h1>`, then these components open at `<h3>`. (`SpeedTierChart.tsx:428`
correctly uses `<h2>`, as does `MatchupSheet.tsx:67` and `MatchupPlanSlide.tsx:193`.)

*A screen-reader user jumping by heading level finds nothing between the page title and the sub-sections.*

**Fix:** promote the three section headings to `<h2>`; their sub-headings already sit at `<h4>` where needed.

### Colour contrast

#### F-3 · The `dark:` variant is decoupled from the dark-mode toggle
`1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)`
`src/app/globals.css:3-4` (no `@custom-variant dark`), `src/app/layout.tsx:97`, `src/hooks/useDarkMode.ts:38-44`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S (one `@custom-variant` line, then verify the 132 call sites) · conflict risk: low (`globals.css`)

Dark mode is applied by setting `data-dark-mode` on `<html>` (blocking script at `layout.tsx:97`, store at
`useDarkMode.ts:38-44`). `globals.css` declares `@custom-variant` for `creator` and `presenting` but **not for
`dark`**, so Tailwind v4's built-in `dark:` keeps its default `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`
implementation. The CSS custom properties under `[data-dark-mode]` (`globals.css:263-287`) and the `dark:`
utilities therefore switch on two independent signals.

Computed consequences for the two mismatch states:

| State | Example class | Rendered pair | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| App dark, OS light | `text-amber-700 dark:text-amber-300` | `#B45309` on `#141428` | **3.60:1** |
| App dark, OS light | `text-rose-600 dark:text-rose-400` | `#E11D48` on `#141428` | **3.85:1** |
| App dark, OS light | `text-blue-600 dark:text-blue-400` | `#2563EB` on `#141428` | **3.50:1** |
| App dark, OS light | `text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-400` | `#9333EA` on `#141428` | **3.36:1** |
| App dark, OS light | `dark:text-slate-*` sibling | `#475569` on `#141428` | **2.39:1** |
| App light, OS dark | `text-amber-700 dark:text-amber-300` | `#FCD34D` on `#FFFFFF` | **1.44:1** |
| App light, OS dark | `dark:text-emerald-400` | `#34D399` on `#FFFFFF` | **1.92:1** |
| App light, OS dark | `dark:text-sky-400` | `#38BDF8` on `#FFFFFF` | **2.14:1** |

The two `.dark`-class selectors already in the stylesheet (`globals.css:597`, `:888`, `:924`) are also dead —
nothing ever adds a `dark` class.

*A user whose laptop is in light mode but who turned on dark mode in the app sees dark-brown "hidden slide" warnings and brand-red labels on near-black panels, at roughly a third of the required contrast.*

**Fix:** add `@custom-variant dark (&:where([data-dark-mode], [data-dark-mode] *));` beside the other two custom
variants, and change the three `.dark`/`:is(.dark)` selectors to `[data-dark-mode]`. Then re-check the ~15 call
sites whose light-mode half is also marginal (F-6).

#### F-5 · White-on-accent is 2.69:1 in dark mode (74 call sites)
`1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)`
`src/components/ui/Button.tsx:16`; `--accent` redefinition at `src/app/globals.css:285`; also e.g. `SlideNavControls.tsx:202,311`, `TeamOverview.tsx:207`, `ShareModal.tsx:487`, `Navbar.tsx:651-655`, the skip link at `layout.tsx:100`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`SlideNavControls.tsx`, `TeamOverview.tsx`)

In light mode `#FFFFFF` on `#E11D48` is 4.70:1 (pass). In dark mode `globals.css:285` sets
`--accent: var(--accent-dark, #FB7185)` and `#FFFFFF` on `#FB7185` is **2.69:1**. The theme table makes it worse
for four of the six accents: `accent-themes.ts:15-19` sets `darkAccent` to `#60A5FA`, `#34D399`, `#FBBF24`,
`#A78BFA`, `#FB923C` — all light tints that need dark, not white, foreground text.

This hits the active section tab in the report's bottom nav, the Mega/Base radio group, every `Button variant="primary"`, and the skip link.

**Fix:** in `Button.tsx` and the shared patterns, pair `bg-accent` with a token (e.g. a new
`--accent-on` / `text-accent-foreground`) that resolves to `#FFFFFF` in light mode and `#1A1A2E` in dark mode,
and set it per theme in `accent-themes.ts`. Then sweep the literal `bg-accent text-white` sites. This overlaps
VGC-297's design call and should be decided with it.

#### F-7 · Semantic tokens that fail in their own mode (VGC-297, widened)
`1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)`
`src/app/globals.css:22-27` (light), `:263-287` (dark), `:30-31` (heatmap cells)
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S (token edit) + design sign-off · conflict risk: low (`globals.css`)

| Token | Pair | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| `--accent` light | `#E11D48` on `--background #FAF9F6` | **4.46:1** |
| `--accent` light | `#E11D48` on `--surface-alt #F2F0EB` | **4.12:1** |
| `--accent` light | `#E11D48` on `--accent-surface #FFF1F2` | **4.28:1** |
| `--success` light | `#16A34A` on any light surface | **2.89–3.30:1** |
| `--warning` light | `#D97706` on any light surface | **2.80–3.19:1** |
| `--danger` light | `#DC2626` on `--surface-alt` | **4.24:1** |
| `--danger` dark | `#DC2626` on `#0B0B1A`/`#141428`/`#1C1C38` | **3.42–4.04:1** |
| `--cell-immune-text` light | `#71717A` on `--cell-immune #E4E4E7` | **3.81:1** |
| `--accent-light` light | `#FDA4AF` on `#FFFFFF` | **1.89:1** |

`--text-primary`, `--text-secondary` and `--text-tertiary` all pass in both modes (5.49:1 worst case), and the
other four heatmap cell pairs pass (5.30–7.30:1) — the token system is sound apart from these.

`--success #0F7A37`, `--warning #A85C04`, `--accent` to ~`#C8143C`; lighten `--danger` in dark mode
(`#F87171` gives 6.54:1); darken `--cell-immune-text` to `#52525B` (5.4:1). Treat `--accent-light` as a
decorative/border-only token and stop using it for text. This is VGC-297's design call — the table above gives
it the numbers to decide from.

#### F-8 · Focus indicator below 3:1 on ~16 controls, including the shared `Button` primitive
`1.4.11 Non-text Contrast`
`src/components/ui/Button.tsx:13` · `PasteInput.tsx:385` · and 15 sites with `focus:outline-none` + `focus:ring-accent/40` and no `focus:border-accent`: `TeamOverview.tsx:197`, `CreatorProfile.tsx:306`, `MatchTracker.tsx:247,322,335`, `ExploreFilters.tsx:425`, `tools/ev-to-sp/page.tsx:350,356`, `EvToSpConverter.tsx:115,123,187`, `DashboardContent.tsx:208,1097,1105,1111`, `ChampionsContent.tsx:484`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** (ratios) / **NEEDS BROWSER VERIFICATION** (whether any site has a second indicator in practice) · effort: S · conflict risk: low–medium (`TeamOverview.tsx` is on the list)

`globals.css:823-828` defines a correct global fallback — `*:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--accent) }`
at 4.70:1 — but it lives in `@layer base`, and Tailwind's `focus:outline-none` utility sits in the
higher-precedence utilities layer, so wherever `outline-none` appears the global ring is cancelled. The
replacement rings are alpha-modified:

| Replacement | Composited | Ratio (needs 3:1) |
|---|---|---|
| `ring-accent/30` | `#F6BBC8` on `#FFFFFF` | 1.63:1 |
| `ring-accent/40` | `#F3A5B6` on `#FFFFFF` | 1.93:1 |
| `ring-accent/50` (`Button.tsx:13`) | `#F08EA4` on `#FFFFFF` | 2.31:1 |
| `border-accent/50` (paste textarea) | `#F08EA4` on `#FFFFFF` | 2.31:1 |

28 of the 43 `ring-accent/40` sites also set `focus:border-accent`, which gives a 4.70:1 2px border and is an
adequate indicator — those are fine. The 58 `focus-visible:ring-accent` sites (full opacity) are also fine.

*A keyboard user tabbing through the dashboard or the EV-to-SP tool cannot see which control they are on.*

**Fix:** drop the `/30`–`/50` alpha from every `ring-accent` (full-opacity accent is 4.70:1 light / 6.72:1 dark),
starting with `Button.tsx:13` since that one covers every `Button` in the app. Better still, delete the
`focus:outline-none` from these class strings and let the `globals.css:823` fallback do the work.

### Keyboard and focus

#### F-9 · Deleting a game plan is mouse-only, and nested inside a `<button>`
`2.1.1 Keyboard` · `4.1.2 Name, Role, Value`
`src/components/report/MatchupPlanSlide.tsx:552-559` (closing `</button>` at `:560`; same block at `:615` has the `title`-only swap button)
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: XS · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`MatchupPlanSlide.tsx`)

```tsx
<span onClick={(e) => { e.stopPropagation(); onDelete(); }} className="…">{t.delete}</span>
```

This `<span>` has no `role`, no `tabIndex` and no key handler, and it sits inside the collapse `<button>` that
wraps the whole game-plan header. A keyboard user can reach the outer button (which toggles collapse) but can
never reach Delete; the nesting is also invalid HTML, so the outer button's accessible name absorbs the word
"Delete".

*A keyboard-only creator cannot delete a game plan, and hears the collapse control announced as "… Delete, button".*

**Fix:** move the delete control out of the `<button>` (sibling in the header flex row) and make it a real
`<button type="button" aria-label={…}>`.

#### F-10 · Editing a damage calc inline is mouse-only

#### F-11 · The coverage-chart type filter is not keyboard operable

#### F-12 · Coverage heatmaps have no header associations
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships`
`DefensiveCoverageChart.tsx:122-205`, `OffensiveCoverageChart.tsx:221-300`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S · conflict risk: low

Both tables use `<th>` without `scope` for the type columns, a plain `<td>` (not `<th scope="row">`) for the
Pokémon name at `DefensiveCoverageChart.tsx:154` / `OffensiveCoverageChart.tsx:253`, and have no `<caption>`.
Cell `title` attributes carry the full meaning (`"Fire → Incineroar: 0.5x"`) but `title` is not a reliable
accessible name on a `<span>`.

For contrast: `ThreatMatrix.tsx:120` does this correctly — `scope="col"`, `scope="row"`, an `sr-only`
`<caption>`, and a per-cell `aria-label` — and is the pattern to copy.

*A screen-reader user navigating the defensive heatmap hears "½x, 2×, 4×, •, •" with no way to tell which Pokémon or which attacking type each value belongs to.*

**Fix:** add `scope="col"` to the type headers, convert the row-name `<td>` to `<th scope="row">`, add an
`sr-only` `<caption>`, and give each cell `<span>` an `aria-label` mirroring its existing `title`.

#### F-13 · Reordering the team is drag-only
`2.1.1 Keyboard`
`src/components/report/TeamOverview.tsx:743-815` (`draggable={canDrag}`, `canDrag` at `:397`)
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: M · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`TeamOverview.tsx`)

`onReorderPokemon` is reachable only through HTML5 drag-and-drop. `LongPressWrapper`'s keydown handler
(`:116-122`) maps Enter/Space to `onTap` (open details) only. Slide reordering *does* have a keyboard path

#### F-14 · Single-character keyboard shortcuts with no way to turn off or remap
`2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts` (Level A)
`src/hooks/useSlideNavigation.ts:142-181`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S · conflict risk: low

`d`, `f`, `p`, `e`, `h`, `?`, `[`, `]`, `0`–`9` are bound on a `window` keydown listener with no modifier.
The guard at `:108-115` only exempts `TEXTAREA`/`INPUT`/`SELECT`/`contentEditable` and a few composite roles —
the shortcuts are live whenever focus is anywhere else on the page, including on `<body>`, which is where focus
sits for most of a reading session. 2.1.4 requires at least one of: a way to turn them off, a way to remap
them, or activation only while the relevant component has focus; none is present. `ShortcutHintOverlay` /
`WalkthroughOverlay` document them but offer no toggle.

*A screen-reader user whose AT passes single keys through to the page flips dark mode on and off, enters presentation mode and reorders slides while trying to read.*

**Fix:** add a "keyboard shortcuts" preference (default on) persisted alongside the other `vgc-*` localStorage
flags, and gate the single-key branch on it; or require a modifier for the mode-changing keys
(`d`/`f`/`p`/`e`/`h`/`[`/`]`) and keep only arrows, Home/End unmodified.

#### F-15 · `role="menu"` on the settings dropdown with no menu items
`4.1.2 Name, Role, Value` · `2.1.1 Keyboard`
`src/components/layout/Navbar.tsx:631` (trigger at `:613-615` with `aria-haspopup="menu"`)
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`Navbar.tsx`)

The container claims `role="menu"` but its children are plain `<div>`s, `<a>`s, `<button>`s, a `Toggle` and a
`LanguageSelector`. There are no `role="menuitem"` children and no arrow-key handling. `role="menu"` puts AT

**Fix:** remove `role="menu"` from `:631` and `aria-haspopup="menu"` from `:614` — the contents are a grouped

(`NotificationBell.tsx:87-94` already does this and is the local precedent).

### Screen-reader semantics

#### F-16 · `role="button"` wrapper makes every overview card's contents presentational
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships` · `4.1.2 Name, Role, Value`
`src/components/report/TeamOverview.tsx:87-124` (`role={onTap ? "button" : undefined}`), applied to each `<PokemonCard>` at `:743-815`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** (the markup and the ARIA rule) / **NEEDS BROWSER VERIFICATION** (how much each AT actually suppresses) · effort: M · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`TeamOverview.tsx`, `PokemonCard.tsx`)

`LongPressWrapper` is correct as a widget in isolation — `role="button"`, `tabIndex={0}`, `aria-label`,
Enter/Space. The problem is what it wraps. ARIA specifies `button` as a role whose children are presentational,
so the card's `<h3>` species name (`PokemonCard.tsx:244`), its type badges, stats, item, moves, the Mega toggle
(`:252-268`) and the damage-calculator link (`:275-296`) are all folded into a single node named
"Open Incineroar details". The card's own controls are focusable DOM nodes inside a `role="button"`, which is
also a conformance error for 4.1.2.

*On the team overview — the first and most-shared slide — a screen-reader user hears six buttons named "Open X details" and none of the team's actual content, and cannot reach the Mega toggles.*

**Fix:** drop `role="button"`/`tabIndex`/`aria-label` from the wrapper and instead put an explicit
"View details" link or button *inside* each card (the damage-calc link at `PokemonCard.tsx:275` shows the
pattern). Keep the touch long-press/tap handlers on the plain `<div>` — they are a touch affordance, not the
only path. This is the single highest-value structural fix in the report.

#### F-17 · `aria-label` on 21 non-semantic elements is silently dropped
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships` · `1.4.1 Use of Color`
`StatsPanel.tsx:97,99,100,155` · `PokemonCard.tsx:442,516,517` · `SlideNavControls.tsx:230` · `SpeedTierChart.tsx:643` · `MetaSnapshot.tsx:134,161,169` · `ReportCard.tsx:374` · `ViewCount.tsx:9` · `CreatorProfile.tsx:163` · `ExploreFilters.tsx:261,325` · `TeamCardExport.tsx:199`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: S · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`PokemonCard.tsx`, `SlideNavControls.tsx`, `SpeedTierChart.tsx`)

`aria-label` only applies to elements with a non-generic role. On a bare `<div>`/`<span>` it is ignored, so
these labels never reach AT. The consequential ones:

- `StatsPanel.tsx:99-100` and `PokemonCard.tsx:516-517` — nature boosts are a `▲`/`▼` glyph coloured
  emerald/rose, with `aria-label="boosted by nature"` on a `<span>`. Nothing conveys it in text, so the
  information is **shape + colour only** (1.4.1). *A screen-reader user cannot tell which stat the nature raises.*
- `SlideNavControls.tsx:230` — the end-of-report state is a `<span aria-label="End of report">` containing only
  a `CheckIcon`, so it has no accessible name at all.
- `SpeedTierChart.tsx:643` — the visible text "TIE" is read, but the `aria-label` naming *which* Pokémon are
  tied is lost. *A screen-reader user is told there is a speed tie but not with whom.*
- `StatsPanel.tsx:97,155` — per-stat and Mega-delta summaries. The individual numbers are read anyway, so the
  loss here is only the summary phrasing.

**Fix:** replace each with `role="img" aria-label="…"` (valid on a `<span>`) or, preferably, a visually-hidden
`<span className="sr-only">` carrying the same text next to the glyph. For `SlideNavControls.tsx:230`, add
`role="status"` or a `sr-only` label.

#### F-18 · Speed-tier and stat toggles do not expose their pressed state
`4.1.2 Name, Role, Value`
`SpeedTierChart.tsx:470-485` ("Mega Forms"), `:486-503` ("Meta Threats")
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: XS · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`SpeedTierChart.tsx`)

Both are latching toggles whose on/off state is communicated purely by background and text colour; neither
carries `aria-pressed`. The codebase gets this right elsewhere — `PokemonSlideHeader.tsx:55`,
`PokemonCard.tsx:259`, and `TeamOverview.tsx:889` all set `aria-pressed`.

*A screen-reader user cannot tell whether meta threats are currently shown in the chart.*

**Fix:** add `aria-pressed={showMegaTiers}` / `aria-pressed={showMetaThreats}`.

#### Not a finding: the speed-tier chart itself is readable
Worth recording, since the brief asked. `SpeedTierChart.tsx:560-690` renders each row's species name
(`:581-585`) and its final speed number (`:650-658`) as real text in descending order, with "+N"/"−N" deltas
carrying an explicit sign and the "TIE" marker as literal text. The bars are purely decorative reinforcement.
A screen-reader user *can* determine the ordering and the numbers; the gaps are F-6 (light-mode contrast),
F-17 (tie partners) and F-18 (toggle state), not the data itself. Likewise `ThreatMatrix.tsx:104-126` is the
best-instrumented visualisation in the codebase.

#### F-19 · Validation errors are announced but not programmatically associated
`1.3.1 Info and Relationships` · `3.3.1 Error Identification`
`PasteInput.tsx:412-419` (error) vs `:368-387` (textarea); `ThreatMatrix.tsx:113,118`; `TeamOverview.tsx:128-200` (UpdateTeamPanel)

### Motion

#### F-20 · `motion/react` animations bypass `prefers-reduced-motion`
Project UI standard ("reduced-motion respected"); maps to `2.3.3 Animation from Interactions` (**AAA**, not AA)
`CreatorProfile.tsx:101` · `ExploreContent.tsx:262-269` · `ExploreEmpty.tsx:30-31` · `ReportCard.tsx:163-166` · `WhatsNewModal.tsx:128-142` · `ChangelogContent.tsx:124,166,270,375` · `MegaLandingContent.tsx:41-42` · `dashboard/profile/page.tsx:4` · `feedback/FeedbackContent.tsx:4`
**CONFIRMED IN CODE** · effort: XS · **HIGH CONFLICT RISK** (`ExploreContent.tsx`)

`globals.css:812-818` correctly neutralises every **CSS** animation and transition under
`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`. But `motion/react` drives its animations through inline
styles/WAAPI, which that media query does not touch, and its `reducedMotion` default is `"never"`. There is no
`<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">` anywhere in `src` (verified), and only **1 of 10** files importing
`motion` consults the preference — `ExploreFilters.tsx:130` via `useReducedMotion()`. So the y-translate
entrances, scale pops, stagger cascades and the `MegaLandingContent.tsx:41` width animation all still play.

`DoubleTapLikeOverlay.tsx:152-158` handles it manually and correctly, which is the other good precedent.

*A user who has asked their OS to reduce motion still gets sliding and scaling content on Explore, the changelog, the mega guides and the What's New modal.*

**Fix:** wrap the app (or each `motion` subtree) in `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">`, which makes
`motion/react` drop transform and layout animations while keeping opacity fades. One provider in
`src/app/layout.tsx` covers all ten files.

#### Not a finding: no 2.2.2 violation found
The only infinite CSS animations are `version-diff-pulse` (`globals.css:884`), `.skeleton` shimmer (`:514`) and
Tailwind's `animate-pulse` on loading placeholders. The latter two are loading indicators (explicitly exempt),
and all three are stopped by the `prefers-reduced-motion` block. The diff-highlight pulse is decorative
box-shadow/opacity with no informational content.

## Method and limits

Static analysis settled every heading, role, attribute and keyboard-handler question above, and every contrast
ratio was computed from declared hex values. It could **not** settle:

- whether `data-export-theme` / `[data-creator-mode]` override chains shift any ratio at runtime (both redefine
  `--surface` and `--border`);
- actual tab order through the fixed bottom nav, the portalled modals and the slide content (DOM order suggests
  it is correct, but `createPortal` in `ShareModal.tsx:317` appends to `<body>`);
- whether focus is lost when slide content unmounts — `SlideNavControls.tsx:418-420` refocuses the dot it moved
  to, the prev/next buttons persist across slides, and `:547-549` is a correct `aria-live` announcement, so
  this looks handled, but it needs a real keyboard run;
- how far each screen reader actually suppresses content inside `role="button"` (F-16);
- contrast of text over the gradient fills (`from-pink-500 to-purple-600` on the Mega toggles — the midpoint
  computes to 3.80:1 for white text, but the true worst case depends on where the glyph sits).

A browser pass with axe-core plus one keyboard-only and one screen-reader run through the report viewer would
close all five.
